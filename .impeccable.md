## Design Context

### Users
The primary users are job seekers—professionals managing multiple high-stakes job applications. They need efficiency, clarity, and a sense of progress. The interface should feel like a "Personal Career Command Center."

### Brand Personality
Orbit is **Precise**, **Kinetic**, and **Editorial**.
- **Precise**: Information is dense but clearly organized, like telemetry data.
- **Kinetic**: The UI feels alive with subtle motion, responsive transitions, and a sense of momentum.
- **Editorial**: Layouts use bold typography, asymmetric spacing, and a focus on high-quality content presentation, reminiscent of a high-end technical dossier.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light-mode primarily, using a "Dossier" aesthetic.
- **Palette**: Deep navy/slate foundations (`--color-primary`) with sharp teal/magenta accents (`--color-accent`). Soft grey/blue surface layers (`--color-surface-container`).
- **Typography**: `Manrope` for bold, technical headlines; `DM Sans` for highly legible body text.
- **Visual Language**: Depth is created through layered surfaces and subtle shadows rather than heavy borders. Layouts are often asymmetric with generous whitespace (32px - 80px gaps).

### Design Principles
1. **Dossier Hierarchy**: Present information as part of an official record. Use clear headers, technical labels, and structured metadata.
2. **Kinetic Feedback**: Every action should have a subtle, high-quality motion response (e.g., cubic-bezier transitions, staggered reveals).
3. **Telemetry Density**: Allow for information density in tables and lists but use typography and spacing to prevent overwhelm.
4. **Editorial Composition**: Use asymmetry and grid-breaking elements to avoid a generic "bootstrap" look.
5. **Surface Layering**: Represent different levels of information depth through background color shifts rather than borders.
